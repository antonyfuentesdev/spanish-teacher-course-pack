Applies to every level exam (A1–C1), the C1 graduation oral and placement · Rules source: 13.9 Rubrics §3, §4, §6 and 13.11 Progress tracking (Assessments, Gates §5–§8). If this page and 13.9 ever differ, 13.9 wins.

This page is the examiner's guide: timings, what the learner is told, how each paper is run, how it is scored, the evidence-quote rules, the retention set and the second scoring pass. The papers themselves are on the level pages (A1–C1 exam papers), each with Forms 1, 2 and 3.

## 1. What the learner is told before the exam (say it in English at A1–A2)

Read this when the learner becomes eligible (13.11 Gates §5), and again at the start of each sitting:

> "Your A1 exam has two parts on two different days. Part 1 is listening and speaking, about 45 minutes, and it ends with a five-minute memory check: 10 words and phrases from this level that you learned at least three weeks ago (you need 7 of 10). Part 2 is reading and writing, about an hour, only if you use the typed track; it decides which badge you get, not whether you move up. There are no hints during the exam. I score it after the call and check my scoring again the next day, so you'll get your result the next day, not right away. If you don't pass a part, you can retake just that part after 10 days, with different questions."

At B1–C1 the bot says the same in Spanish (with the level's timings from §2), and repeats in English on request.

At the start of every paper: "Ahora es una prueba." (A1–A2: "Now it's a short test: no hints until the end.") Then the paper's instructions, once, slowly.

## 2. Timings

One exam paper group per day at most (13.11 Gates §1). Group 2 (listening + speaking + retention set) is one sitting; Group 1 (reading + writing) is another day. A sitting may pause once for up to 10 minutes between papers.

| Level | Listening | Speaking (prep + talk) | Retention set | Group 2 sitting | Reading | Writing (dictation + tasks) | Group 1 sitting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 20 min | 5 + 10 min | 5 min | about 45 min | 25 min | 5 + 25 min | about 55 min |
| A2 | 25 min | 8 + 12 min | 5 min | about 55 min | 30 min | 5 + 30 min | about 65 min |
| B1 | 30 min | 10 + 15 min | 5 min | about 65 min | 40 min | 5 + 45 min (2 tasks) | about 90 min |
| B2 | 35 min | 15 + 18 min | 5 min | about 75 min | 50 min | 5 + 55 min (2 tasks) | about 110 min |
| C1 | 40 min | 20 + 20 min | 5 min | about 90 min | 55 min | 5 + 60 min (2 tasks) | about 120 min |

Times are targets, not cut-offs, for listening and speaking (the bot reads at the level's pace and never rushes an answer; after 20 seconds of silence it says "¿Pasamos a la siguiente?" and an unanswered item scores 0). Reading and writing have the stated time; at the limit the bot asks for the final answer.

## 3. Running each paper

<b>Listening (25 items, 1 point each)</b>
- Five tasks of five items. The bot reads the task instruction, then the questions, then the clip twice, then waits for the answers. The learner answers aloud.
- Clip speed follows 13.8: A1 slow and short sentences; A2 slow-normal; B1 normal; B2 natural; C1 natural with some fast passages.
- <b>Options:</b> at most 3 options per item at every level. At A1–A2, options are 1–3 words, read twice; the learner may answer with the letter, the word, or their own short answer with the same meaning; the learner may ask once per item to hear the options again (not the clip). Short-answer items accept any answer with the key's meaning; spelling and grammar are not scored in listening.
- Only the content of the key counts. A partly right answer to a two-part item scores 0 unless the key says "either".

<b>Speaking (25 points: 5 criteria × 0–5, 13.9 §3)</b>
- Task 1 monologue (after the preparation time; the learner may make notes), Task 2 conversation (the bot asks the fixed questions on the paper, in order; it may ask one follow-up "¿Por qué?" or "¿Y qué más?" per question), Task 3 role play (the bot plays the role card on the paper, using its fixed turns; it adapts only to keep the conversation possible).
- Score the three tasks together, once per criterion, on the 13.9 §3 scale, using the level's anchors on the level page.
- Pronunciation is not scored. "Understood" means the message got through, not accent.

<b>Reading (25 items, 1 point each; typed track)</b>
- Texts shown in the chat, one task at a time. Answers in chat or aloud: a letter or a short answer. Spelling is not scored in reading.

<b>Writing (25 points; typed track)</b>
- <b>Dictation (5 points):</b> the bot reads the whole text once, then each marked chunk ( / ) twice, then the whole text again. The learner types. Score = 5 minus 1 for every two spelling or accent errors (1 error = −0, 2–3 = −1, 4–5 = −2, …; minimum 0). A missing word counts as one error; capital letters and punctuation are not counted.
- <b>Tasks (20 points):</b> each task is scored on task achievement, organization, range and accuracy, 0–5 each (20). With two tasks, the writing-task score is the mean of the two task totals, rounded down. Word ranges are on each task; under the lower limit, task achievement is at most 2.

## 4. Evidence-quote rules

Every point given or withheld cites the transcript (13.9 §6). In the Evidence block of the Assessments row:

- Listening and reading: one line per item with the learner's exact answer: `- L7 (1/1): "a las ocho"` · `- R12 (0/1): "b" (key c)`
- Speaking: one quote per criterion, the exact words that justify the band; for any score under 5, add a second quote showing the shortfall: `- S Accuracy (3/5): "Fui al mercado y compré" (ok) / "cuando era niño fui en Lima" (error in core past use)`
- Writing: one quote per criterion per task, plus the dictation error list: `- Dictado (4/5): "esta" for "está", "despues" for "después"`
- Retention set: one line per item with the prompt and the answer.
- Never paraphrase. A point without a quote is not given. Silence is recorded as "(no answer)". Re-asks for an unclear ending (13.8) are noted: at most 2 per paper.

## 5. Retention set ("exam memory check", 10 items, 7/10 needed)

Required by 13.11 Gates §5 on top of the paper rule. Learner name: "exam memory check".

1. <b>Draw (before the sitting, written into the Evidence block first so it can't change):</b> 10 items of this level from the Review queue and the archive, created at least 21 days before the exam date. Spread: at most 2 items per topic; at least 4 archived (Done) items if the archive has them; at most 3 Error items; skip items reviewed in the last 7 days when possible.
2. <b>If fewer than 10 items qualify:</b> fill with the ★ fast-track seeds of this level's topics passed at least 21 days earlier (each topic page, §8). If still fewer than 10, the exam cannot run yet; the bot tells the learner when it can ("in 5 days, when more of your items are three weeks old").
3. <b>Run:</b> at the end of the Group 2 sitting, after speaking, about 5 minutes. Same prompt style as review calls (English meaning → Spanish at A1–A2; Spanish definition or gap at B1–C1), no hints, one answer per item.
4. <b>Score:</b> 1 point each; the answer must match the queue item's target form (meaning plus the target ending or chunk). 7/10 or more passes.
5. <b>Results:</b> written in Scores as "Retention 8/10". The Group 2 result is Pass only if listening + speaking ≥ 30/50 <b>and</b> retention ≥ 7/10. Missed items go back to the queue at step 1.
6. If the papers pass but the retention set does not, only the retention set is retaken, at least 10 days later, with a fresh draw.

The 30-day retention check after a level pass (13.11 Gates §7, 6/10) is a different check: the "one-month check". Under 6/10 the learner gets "a refresher before your next exam"; the badge is never marked.

## 6. Second scoring pass

- <b>Pass 1:</b> right after the call, from the transcript, using the key and rubric on the level page. Write the scores and the Evidence block; Result stays "pending" and the learner is told: "I'll check my scoring tomorrow and give you your result then."
- <b>Pass 2:</b> the next day, by the daily reconcile routine, from the transcript, key and rubric only. Score everything again without opening pass 1's scores, then compare.
- <b>Rule:</b> if a group total differs by more than 5 points, or pass/fail differs (group or retention set), the lower result stands and the row is flagged "Passes disagree". Otherwise pass 1 stands and pass 2 goes in "Pass 2 total".
- Note any single speaking or writing criterion that differs by 2 or more between passes in the Evidence block ("Calibration note"), even when the totals agree; these are kept for the bot's own scoring-consistency review.
- Only after pass 2 does the bot tell the learner the result, write the Milestones row (Level up / Badge, citing the Assessments ID) and update the percentages.

## 7. Results, records and retakes

- Assessments row: Type Level exam, Level, Form 1/2/3, Attempt, Scores "Listening 20/25 · Speaking 18/25 · Reading 15/25 · Writing 17/25 · Retention 8/10", Total "G2 38/50 · G1 32/50", Result, Pass 2 total, Flag, Earliest retake, Evidence.
- Pass rule: each group ≥ 30/50 (papers added together inside a group). <b>Only Group 2 (listening + speaking) plus the exam memory check moves the learner up</b>; Group 1 (reading + writing) decides only which badge is awarded, and failing or skipping it never blocks a level up (13.9 §3).
- Badges (13.9 §3): Group 2 passed → "Spoken Spanish [level] — listening and speaking (bot-assessed)"; Groups 2 and 1 passed → "Spanish [level] — listening, speaking, reading and writing (bot-assessed)". Never DELE, SIELE or an official CEFR certificate. The C1 exam is "inspired by DELE C1".
- Forms: first sitting Form 1, second Form 2, third Form 3 (for each group separately). Retake the failed group only, at least 10 days later, on the next form; at most 3 sittings, then a 4-week review cycle; after it, Form 1 again.
- What the learner hears at the result (English at A1–A2): the two group totals, the retention score, two strengths, two focus areas, and, if a group failed, the earliest retake date.

## 8. Content rules for all papers

- Neutral Latin American Spanish (MX, CR, CO, PA), as in 13.8; regional words are labelled and accepted.
- All people, companies, events, prices and figures in the papers are fictional. Real place names (cities, countries) appear only as settings, with no factual claims about them. No real statistics.
- Papers are fixed: nothing is improvised at test time (13.9 §6). Forms are never shown to the learner before the sitting, and exam texts are never reused in lessons.
