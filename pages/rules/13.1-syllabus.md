All 16 courses and 48 topics, A1 to C1.

<b>Provenance:</b> topic names are the official AVE Global names (Instituto Cervantes). The grammar, lexis, chunks and sounds per topic are <b>our own assignment</b>. They are <b>not</b> mapped to the Instituto Cervantes Plan Curricular inventories (those pages could not be opened); a PCIC check is future work. C1.4 topic 10 grammar comes from the Cervantes Berlin C1 topic list (search snippet).

<b>How to read a row:</b> each topic has <b>one main grammar focus</b> (taught explicitly and checked in the topic check) plus <b>supporting items</b> (extra grammar, lexis and fixed chunks the topic's functions need; taught as chunks or short notes, practised, not separately examined). One sound or prosody feature per topic. Full, unstressed vowels are recycled in every A1–A2 drill.

<b>Content variety:</b> neutral Latin American Spanish that works across Mexico, Costa Rica, Colombia and Panama: ustedes (no vosotros), seseo, yeísmo, indefinido for recent finished events, and vocabulary common to all four countries. Region-only words are never default teaching forms; when one appears it is labelled with the countries that use it (regional-awareness table in 13.8). Spain is mentioned only for contrast.

## Vocabulary targets

- Each topic has an active list (drilled and reviewed) and a passive list (heard, recognized, not drilled)
- Active items per topic: A1–A2 about 30–40; B1 about 40–50; B2–C1 about 50–60, including collocations and chunks
- Cumulative active target: roughly 450 after A2, 1,000 after B1, 1,700 after B2, 2,400 after C1 (our estimate); retired items move to the archive file (13.10)
- <b>If review blocks are skipped</b> and new items drop to 2 per lesson session (13.10), the topic is not extended: its active target is lowered to what was introduced and recorded in the Profile field "Lowered active targets" (13.11)
- "Words learned" in 13.11 counts active items that reached the last review step
- The full per-topic lists are part of the materials checklist in 13.2; only the five worked examples have lists so far

## Hours and pacing

<b>Bot lesson core:</b> A1–A2 5 h, B1 6 h, B2 7 h, C1 8 h per topic, plus 1 h of consolidation per course: <b>328 h, or 656 lesson sessions of 30 minutes</b> (AVE Global plans about 10 h per topic, about 480 h). This is the bot's <b>lesson core, not the whole learning time</b>. By published guided-learning-hour benchmarks, 328 guided hours sits around the <b>B1</b> band, not C1.

| Level (cumulative) | Plan: bot lesson hours | Cambridge English support page | Cambridge University Press 2018, adult example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 32 | 90–100 | 90–100 |
| A2 | 64 | 180–200 | 190–250 |
| B1 | 140 | 350–400 | 350–490 |
| B2 | 228 | 500–600 | 530–750 |
| C1 | 328 | 700–800 | 730–1,050 |

- Both Cambridge sources count guided learning hours as classroom plus guided self-study; the 2018 paper adds that higher-level learners "need a smaller proportion of their language learning hours to be in the classroom or teacher-directed" — [Cambridge English: Guided learning hours](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours), [Cambridge University Press: How long does it take to learn a foreign language? (PDF, 2018)](https://www.cambridge.org/elt/blog/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/How-long-does-it-take-to-learn-a-foreign-language.pdf)
- The U.S. Foreign Service Institute lists Spanish at 30 weeks (Category I: 24–30 weeks, 552–690 class hours) for an ILR 3 in speaking and listening, with "23 hours per week in class and 17 hours of self-study" — [U.S. Department of State: Foreign Language Training](https://www.state.gov/foreign-language-training/). ILR does not map exactly onto CEFR
- <b>Learning hours</b> (what the plan counts) = bot lessons + review blocks + typed track + exams + free talk + self-reported outside listening, reading and conversation: the sum of all Sessions minutes (13.11). <b>Lesson hours</b> = Lesson + Consolidation minutes only, used by the gates

<b>Planning totals (ours, cumulative learning hours):</b> A1 70–100 · A2 150–200 · B1 300–400 · B2 450–600 · C1 650–800, at or just below the Cambridge figures. The bot's lesson share is about 100% at A1–A2 and falls to about 50% at B2–C1.

<b>Note:</b> the 650–800 h C1 figure is the external research benchmark (Cambridge guided learning hours), kept for internal pace checks only; the course frame itself is the 328 guided lesson hours above, about 1–2 years at 6 h a week (about 1 year if every hour were a lesson, about 2 years under the 1-lesson-hour-a-day cap).

<b>Expected duration (internal side metric, never a learner promise)</b>

Used by the bot for sanity checks and anti-rush logic only (13.11 Pace). Learners see their level, Level % and Overall % instead (13.11), because study patterns vary a lot: a learner who studies every day can legitimately move faster.

| Finish level | Bot lesson core only (fastest weeks under the lesson cap, at 3 / 6 / 10 h a week) | 3 h a week | 6 h a week (e.g. 2 h × 3 days) | 10 h a week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 32 h → 11 / 11 / 6.4 | 23–33 weeks (5–8 months) | 12–17 weeks (3–4 months) | 7–10 weeks (about 2 months) |
| A2 | 64 h → 21 / 21 / 13 | 50–67 weeks (12–15 months) | 25–33 weeks (6–8 months) | 15–20 weeks (3.5–4.5 months) |
| B1 | 140 h → 47 / 47 / 28 | 100–133 weeks (2–2.5 years) | 50–67 weeks (12–15 months) | 30–40 weeks (7–9 months) |
| B2 | 228 h → 76 / 76 / 46 | 150–200 weeks (3–4 years) | 75–100 weeks (17–23 months) | 45–60 weeks (10–14 months) |
| C1 | 328 h → 110 / 110 / 66 | 217–267 weeks (4–5 years) | 108–133 weeks (25–31 months) | 65–80 weeks (15–19 months) |

- The "bot lesson core only" column is the fastest the content can be delivered, not a learning estimate. It now respects the cap of 2 lesson sessions (1 lesson hour) a day, assuming 3 h a week = 3 days × 1 h (3 lesson h), 6 h a week = 3 days × 2 h (still 3 lesson h, the rest review and practice) and 10 h a week = 5 days × 2 h (5 lesson h). The other columns divide the planning totals (all learning hours) by hours per week
- Learners never see this table or any range from it, even when they ask how long a level takes; they get Level %, Overall % and what is left in the level instead (13.11 Motivation)
- Reading at 6 h a week: 1–2 years fits B1–B2; C1 needs roughly 2–2.5 years of total learning
- The bot never quotes these figures to a learner as a date. At intake and in its weekly pace check it uses them internally to flag rushing (far ahead of the table) or a stall (far behind), and may suggest a different study plan
