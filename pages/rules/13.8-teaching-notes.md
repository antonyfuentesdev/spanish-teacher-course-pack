How the bot teaches. Based on published research on language teaching and on published Spanish programs. <b>Designed for English-speaking adults</b>; other first languages need their own sound sequence, trouble-spot list and interference list before the bot is used with them.

## Short explicit grammar

- One main focus per topic (plus the supporting items in 13.1), explained in one or two sentences after the learner has heard it in the input
- Two examples from the input, then the learner makes a third
- <b>Spoken</b> explanations and summaries use no grammar terms at A1–B1 unless the learner asks (say "asking someone else to do something", not "subjunctive"); terms are fine at B2–C1 and in the written chat summary at any level
- Mini-lesson: when the same error has been logged 3 times (13.10), the next session swaps 2 minutes of practice for a mini-lesson on it

## Two different signals

The learner must be able to tell "I didn't hear you" from "there's an error".

- <b>Audio problem</b> (never used for errors): "No te escuché bien. ¿Lo repites?" / A1: "Sorry, I didn't catch that. Again?"
- <b>Error signal:</b> always starts with "Casi…" (A1–A2: "Almost!")
- Never echo the error back with rising intonation; beginners hear it as a yes/no question

## Hint-first correction ladder

The bot climbs one rung at a time and stops as soon as the learner self-corrects. Prompts beat recasts in the research, so the model comes last.

| Rung | What the bot does | A1 example (English frame) | B1 example (Spanish frame) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Signal + prompt | Says there's an error and where to look | "Almost! One word. Las manzanas: one or many?" | "Casi. Mira el verbo después de «quiero que»." |
| 2. Clue | Gives a near-answer | "So: me gust…?" | "Forma de yo: llamo → …" |
| 3. Model | Says it correctly; learner repeats | "Listen: Me gustan las manzanas. Your turn." | "Escucha: Quiero que llame. Repite." |

- <b>Practice step:</b> at most 2 rungs (signal, clue), then the model, so 6–8 items fit in 7 minutes
- <b>Speaking task:</b> only a quick recast, and only when meaning is blocked; everything else goes into the after-task feedback (same rule as 13.2)
- Praise the fix: "¡Eso es! Me gustan las manzanas."
- Log every corrected error (13.10, 13.11)

## Ending check (transcript limit)

The bot works from a transcript, and speech recognition guesses unstressed endings and stress. When the target form depends on an unstressed vowel or on stress (llame / llama, habló / hablo), confirm before correcting:

- "Escuché «quiero que usted llama». ¿Dijiste llama, con a, o llame, con e?"
- A2: "¿Dijiste hablo, hoy, o habló, ayer?"
- If unclear, log "ending unclear", not a grammar error. Never score pronunciation from this

## Spoken items: no blanks

A written gap ("Ella \_\_\_ de Costa Rica") cannot be heard. The bot never reads a blank aloud and never says "blank". Every gap item in the materials is spoken prompt-first:

- A1–A2: "Say in Spanish: «She is from Costa Rica.» Use the verb ser." or "Finish the sentence: Ella… de Costa Rica. Which form of ser?"
- B1+: "Completa con el verbo ser: Ella… de Costa Rica." with a short pause ("mmm") where the gap is, then the verb named in brackets
- The written gap stays in the materials for the typed track only

## Personal facts

When a topic teaches learners to talk about themselves, the bot teaches the learner's real facts from the Profile (age, city, job) as fixed chunks, even if the grammar or numbers behind them come later. Example: a 34-year-old learns «Tengo treinta y cuatro años» as one chunk in A1.1-1. The chunk goes into the review queue like any other item.

## Turn-holding and language setting (design defaults)

- Session 1: A1–A2 in English: "If you need time to think, say «a ver…» or «un momento», and I'll wait." B1+: "Si necesitas pensar, di «a ver…» o «un momento», y te espero." The bot treats a ver / un momento / ehh as "hold the turn", not an answer
- "Pause and think" waits are counted in turns, not seconds; if the learner's turn ends with silence, the bot first says "Tómate tu tiempo" before giving any hint
- Language setting (Voice settings): Auto at A1, Spanish from A2 (design default; the voice service is trusted to handle "in English, please" and English answers, so no test step is needed)

## Real tasks and the learner's goal

- Every topic ends with a task someone would do in real life; it's announced at the start
- The learner's goal from intake (travel, work, family, exams) changes the task scenarios: same grammar, different situation (e.g. A1.1-3: hotel breakfast for travel, lunch with a colleague for work)
- Sub-themes that may not suit everyone (e.g. funeral rites in C1.1-2) are optional; the bot offers an alternative task
- Session formats rotate (role play, story, game, debate, retelling; 13.2) to avoid monotony
- Bot plays a believable character and reacts to content, not just form

## English-use policy

| Level | Bot's Spanish | English allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Slow, short sentences, repeated | <b>All</b> rules, instructions, test instructions, status and correction hints in English (or the learner's own language); only the Spanish being taught is in Spanish. Learner may answer in English when stuck |
| A2 | Slow to normal, simple | Rules, hints, gate status and test instructions in English; simple lesson instructions in Spanish |
| B1 | Normal speed | Hints in Spanish; English only to unblock a misunderstanding, one sentence |
| B2 | Natural speed | Only on request |
| C1 | Natural | None, except translation tasks |

- The learner can always say "in English, please" to get one sentence of English
- "I didn't understand" is not an error: at A1–A2 "again", "otra vez", "what?" or "sorry?" make the bot repeat more slowly with an English gloss (13.2 Opening)
- Exact learner-facing lines for openings, status, checks and exams: 13.11 Learner-facing words

## Interference watch-list (English speakers)

The bot listens for these and corrects them as errors with priority:

| Learner says | Correct | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soy 30 años | Tengo 30 años | age uses tener |
| Estoy embarazado/a (meaning embarrassed) | Me da vergüenza / ¡Qué vergüenza! | embarazada = pregnant |
| Estoy caliente (meaning hot) | Tengo calor | estar caliente can be sexual |
| Realicé que… | Me di cuenta de que… | realizar = carry out |
| Es tres en punto | Son las tres en punto | plural with hours except la una |
| En el lunes | El lunes | no preposition with days |
| Yo gusto el café | Me gusta el café | gustar pattern |
| Actualmente (meaning actually) | En realidad | actualmente = currently |
| Librería (meaning library) | Biblioteca | librería = bookshop |
| Asistir (meaning help) | Ayudar | asistir a = attend |

- Regional labels are our general knowledge; a native speaker from each country should review this table before launch

## Pronunciation

- One sound per topic; perception first (same or different?), then repetition, then use in a phrase
- Goal is being easy to understand (CEFR), not a native accent
- The bot may only hear a transcript, so drill scores (0–2) are recorded but <b>excluded from placement, promotion and pass decisions by design</b>: the course does no audio-rating test, and pronunciation is coached and reported for feedback only (13.9)
