How the bot places, checks, tests and graduates learners. Built on SIELE (adaptive placement, score bands), DELE (exam papers, combined 60% pass, C1 oral tasks) and the CEFR phonology scales.

<b>Where results go:</b> every placement, topic check, level exam sitting, retention check and graduation oral is one row in the <b>Assessments</b> table, with its Evidence block of the progress file on the bot's computer (columns in 13.11; "Where progress lives" in the Learning plan overview). Badges and level-ups are Milestones rows. Nothing is kept anywhere else.

<b>Transcript limit:</b> the bot may only hear a transcript. By design, pronunciation is <b>excluded from placement, topic checks, level-exam pass decisions and promotion</b>: the course does no audio-rating test, and pronunciation is coached and reported for feedback only.

## 1. Placement (adaptive, \~15 min, SIELE style)

- One multilevel test instead of one per level. Five ladder steps, two prompts each: greetings and personal facts (A1) → daily routine (A2) → last weekend and changes (B1) → plans, wishes and hypotheses (B2) → defend an opinion on an abstract topic (C1)
- <b>Stop rule:</b> if the learner struggles with a step's first prompt, the bot gives its second prompt; two struggles at the <b>same</b> step end the ladder
- Short listening check: 3 clips at rising speed with questions. <b>Stop rule:</b> clip 2 is played only if the ladder reached step 2 or clip 1 was understood; clip 3 only if the ladder reached step 3 or clip 2 was understood. A beginner never hears the natural-speed clip
- <b>Complete beginner path:</b> the test starts with "Have you studied Spanish before? If not, we can skip the test and start at lesson one." A learner who says no skips the test and starts A1.1-1 (Assessments row type Placement, level A1, Flag "Beginner (test skipped)"). The bot speaks English to this learner from the first line
- Four skills scored 1–5 for the report (speaking, listening, vocabulary, grammar); pronunciation noted but not scored

| Score | Speaking | Listening | Vocabulary | Grammar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Single words, memorized phrases | Slow, familiar words | Survival words | Almost no control |
| 2 | Short sentences on familiar topics | Short clear exchanges | Everyday topics, gaps | Present mostly correct |
| 3 | Connected talk on familiar topics | Main points of clear standard speech | Wide on familiar topics | Past and future used; subjunctive errors |
| 4 | Fluent on most topics, argues a view | Extended speech at natural speed | Broad, some idioms | Subjunctive mostly correct |
| 5 | Flexible, precise, well structured | Implicit meaning, varied accents | Broad, nuanced, register | Consistent control |

<b>Placement rule</b>

- The highest step handled comfortably sets the level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1). If no step is handled comfortably, the level is A1 and the learner starts at A1.1-1 with the normal A1 rules (not "placed"; 13.11 Gates)
- The learner starts at the <b>first course of that level</b> and may fast-track each topic at its session 1 (Form A, 8/10 or more and Part B 4/4; 13.11 Gates §3)
- The learner can always ask to start lower
- Record: one Assessments row, type Placement: level, start course, the four skill scores, failed items (also added to the review queue), with an Evidence block quoting the learner's answer to each prompt
- <b>Locked levels:</b> if the placed level's topics are not in this course pack (13.12 Topic materials), the learner starts at the highest ready level below it, or has free conversation labelled as such (13.11 Gates §10)
- <b>Confirmation:</b> placement only opens fast-tracks. If the first two assessed results at a placed level (fast-track or topic check) are both under 6/10, the bot <b>offers</b> a move down one level; the learner decides, and is never moved automatically. One fast-track under 8/10 just means the topic is taught normally (13.11 Gates §3)

<b>Definitions</b>

- <b>Struggle</b> (one prompt): after one rephrase, no relevant answer; or more than half of the step's target forms wrong; or the answer is mostly in English
- <b>Handled comfortably</b> (one step): both prompts answered relevantly, at least half of the target forms correct, at most one rephrase in the step

<b>Scripts: the finished placement script is </b><b>Placement test</b> (fixed rephrases, the complete-beginner branch, the clip stop rule and result wording). The bot runs that page. The prompts and clips below are the summary it was built from; if they ever differ, 13.13.1 wins. Instructions around the prompts are in English for anyone who has not handled step 2 (13.8)

| Step | Target forms | Prompt 1 | Prompt 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (A1) | ser, tener, present -ar | "¿Cómo te llamas y de dónde eres?" | "¿Cuántos años tienes y dónde vives?" |
| 2 (A2) | present of routine verbs, reflexives, time | "Cuéntame un día normal para ti: ¿a qué hora te levantas y qué haces?" | "¿Qué haces los fines de semana?" |
| 3 (B1) | indefinido + imperfect, pretérito perfecto | "¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana pasado?" | "¿Cómo era tu vida hace diez años y qué ha cambiado?" |
| 4 (B2) | conditional, imperfect subjunctive, present subjunctive after esperar que | "¿Qué harías si tuvieras un año libre?" | "¿Qué esperas que cambie en tu ciudad o en tu trabajo en los próximos años?" |
| 5 (C1) | argument, concession, register | "Algunas personas creen que el teletrabajo debería ser un derecho. ¿Qué opinas? Defiende tu postura." | "Yo pienso lo contrario: que cada empresa debe decidir. Convénceme." |

| Clip | Speed | Text (bot reads it twice) | Questions (answer key) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Slow | "Hola, soy Marta. Vivo en San José, en Costa Rica. Trabajo en un hotel y los domingos voy al mercado con mi hermana." | ¿Dónde trabaja Marta? (en un hotel) · ¿Qué hace los domingos? (va al mercado con su hermana) |
| 2 | Normal | "Ayer quise ir al cine, pero cuando llegué ya no había entradas. Al final me quedé en casa y vi una serie que me recomendó un amigo. La verdad, me gustó más de lo que pensaba." | ¿Por qué no vio la película? (no había entradas) · ¿Qué le pareció la serie? (le gustó más de lo que pensaba) |
| 3 | Natural | "Aunque el gobierno anunció que el transporte público sería gratis para estudiantes, muchas familias dicen que no han notado la diferencia, porque las rutas que usan sus hijos siguen sin funcionar los fines de semana." | ¿Qué se anunció? (transporte gratis para estudiantes) · ¿Por qué las familias no notan la diferencia? (las rutas no funcionan los fines de semana) |

Listening score for the report: clips understood (both questions right) out of 3.

## 2. Topic check (used 48 times)

<b>Template:</b> 10 points, pass 6 (60%), about 8 minutes, in the last session of the topic.

| Part | Items | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Controlled | 5 spoken items on the main focus and supporting chunks (translate, transform, answer) + 1 listening item | 6 (1 each, expected answer in the materials) |
| B. Speaking task | The topic's role play | 4 (1 per criterion) |

Speaking-task criteria (adapt the wording per topic):

1. Completes the task (gets what they needed)
2. Uses the main focus correctly at least twice
3. Responds relevantly to two bot questions or turns
4. Understood with no more than one repetition (understandability of the message, not accent)

- <b>Fixed forms:</b> every topic has Forms A, B, C and D written in its materials (13.2, 13.12). Items are never generated at test time; a topic without its four forms cannot be checked
- <b>Unseen items:</b> at least 3 of the 5 spoken Part A items in each form are unseen (not used in any session exercise, review seed or other form), so the check tests transfer, not recall
- <b>Use of forms:</b> Form A is also the fast-track form. Normal order: regular check Form A, retakes Form B, then Form C. After a failed fast-track (which used Form A): regular check Form B, retakes Form C, then Form D, so no form is reused for that learner. After a third fail: 2 recycle sessions, then one final sitting on Form D if the learner has not used it; only a learner who has already used all four forms gets the least recently used form, flagged "Reused form". Retake limits and spacing: 13.11 Gates §2
- <b>"Seen" and "unseen" marks</b> describe a learner who did the topic's sessions; for a fast-tracker every item is new. Fast-track Form A items must still be answerable from the topic sheet alone, never from a later topic
- <b>A1–A2 listening items:</b> at most 3 short options, read twice, or a short spoken answer
- <b>When:</b> the last session of the topic: 10 min warm-up review, about 8 min check, then feedback and free chat (13.2 session 10)
- Ending-unclear items (13.8) are re-asked once, not marked wrong; at most 2 such re-asks per check, each noted in the Evidence block
- Record: one Assessments row, type Topic check: form, attempt, fast-track yes/no, Part A and Part B scores, skill scores, total, result, second-pass total, failed items (also added to the review queue), a one-line can-do note, and an Evidence block with a quote for every point given or withheld
- Worked sample: A1.1-3 topic check in 13.3

## 3. End-of-level exam (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1)

Four papers, 25 points each, in two groups as in DELE.

| Group | Paper | Format | Who takes it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 (core) | Listening | Spoken clips at level speed; 25 items, 1 point each; spoken answers (multiple choice read out, short answers) | Everyone |
| 2 (core) | Speaking | 3 tasks: monologue, conversation with the bot, role play (DELE task types) | Everyone |
| 1 (typed track) | Reading | Texts shown in the chat; 25 items answered in chat or aloud | Typed-track learners |
| 1 (typed track) | Writing | Typed in chat: (a) a short dictation — the bot speaks, the learner types (spelling and accents), 5 points; (b) one or two writing tasks at level (message, email, opinion text), 20 points | Typed-track learners |

<b>Pass rule (decided): combined, like real DELE.</b> At least 60% in each group, with the two papers in a group added together: listening + speaking ≥ 30 of 50; and, for typed-track learners, reading + writing ≥ 30 of 50. A strong paper can make up for a weak one inside its group, not across groups.

<b>What moves the learner up:</b> only Group 2 (listening + speaking) plus the exam memory check. Group 1 (reading + writing) decides only which badge is awarded; failing or skipping it never blocks a level up.

<b>Papers and length:</b> the papers (3 forms per level, scripts, texts, answer keys) are in 13.13 Exam papers; its timings table gives each group's length (e.g. A1 Group 2 about 45 minutes), and the bot tells the learner the length before starting. A1–A2 listening uses at most 3 short options, read twice, or short spoken answers.

<b>Badge wording (honest):</b>

- Group 2 passed: "Spoken Spanish \[level\] — listening and speaking (bot-assessed)"
- Group 2 + Group 1 passed: "Spanish \[level\] — listening, speaking, reading and writing (bot-assessed)"
- Never called a DELE, SIELE or official CEFR certificate

<b>Speaking scoring (25 points: 5 criteria × 0–5)</b>

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task achievement | All task points covered, appropriate to the situation | Most points covered | Task barely attempted |
| Coherence and fluency | Connected, few hesitations for the level | Some breakdowns, recovers | Isolated words or phrases |
| Range | Varied vocabulary and structures for the level | Adequate but repetitive | Very limited |
| Accuracy | Level's main structures mostly correct | Frequent errors, meaning clear | Errors block meaning |
| Interaction | Starts, responds, repairs naturally | Responds but rarely starts | Needs constant support |

4 and 2 sit between the descriptors. Pronunciation is not scored here (excluded by design; feedback only).

<b>Writing scoring (typed track, 20 points):</b> task achievement, organization, range, accuracy (each 0–5, same bands as above). Dictation: 5 points, minus 1 per two spelling or accent errors.

- <b>Eligibility:</b> only when every gate in 13.11 Gates §5 is met (topics, consolidation, minimum lesson hours, 3 weeks at the level, the previous level's one-month check) and the level's papers are in 13.13. The bot states the gate status before offering the exam
- <b>Exam memory check</b> (the retention set inside every level exam): 10 items of this level created at least 21 days earlier, from the queue or the archive; 7/10 or more is required in addition to the group rule. Learner name: "exam memory check"; the separate 30-day <b>one-month check</b> (6/10) is in 13.11 Gates §7
- <b>Typed-track minimum before Group 1 can be sat</b> (graded writing tasks completed at this level, ours): A1 4 · A2 5 · B1 6 · B2 8 · C1 10, including 2 mediation texts at C1. Each topic's materials include one graded writing task (13.2), done in a named session (13.2) and recorded as an Assessments row, type Writing task; the weekly summary shows "writing tasks x of n"
- Record: one Assessments row per sitting, type Level exam: form, attempt, every paper score, the retention set, each group's total, pass/fail per group, second-pass total, and an Evidence block (one quote per speaking and writing criterion). A retake is a new row. A pass also adds a Milestones row with the badge wording, citing the Assessments ID
- Fail → targeted review from the error log (Error items in the review queue); retake only the failed group, at least 10 days later, on a new form; at most 3 sittings, then a 4-week review cycle (13.11 Gates §6)
- <b>Exam papers:</b> in 13.13 Exam papers (scripts, texts, answer keys; Forms 1–3 per level, with the administration and scoring guide). A level exam runs only while its papers are in this course pack (13.11 Gates §10)
- Source: [DELE structure](https://examenes.cervantes.es/es/dele/como)

## 4. C1 graduation exam

Modelled on the DELE C1 oral paper as described in the official DELE C1 exam guide (2024): 20 minutes plus 20 minutes' preparation, three tasks. The live Cervantes pages returned server errors on Oct 21, 2026; the structure below was checked against the search-index copy of the official guide.

1. <b>Presentation (with mediation):</b> the learner gets a topic and a short source (the bot narrates it, or text in chat on the typed track), prepares, then presents for 3–5 minutes and answers questions
2. <b>Interview</b> on the topic: opinion, hypothesis, defending a view
3. <b>Informal negotiation:</b> reach an agreement with the bot from different positions (part of the real DELE C1 oral test, so required here)

- <b>Sources and topics</b> (3 sets): in C1 graduation oral
- Plus the C1 end-of-level exam (§3) and its combined rule
- Note: the real DELE C1 uses integrated papers (reading and listening with use of language, written mediation) rather than A1–B2-style separate papers; our C1 level exam keeps the §3 format and is called "inspired by DELE C1"
- Record: one Assessments row, type Graduation, plus a Graduation Milestones row citing it (details below) — [DELE C1 exam guide (PDF)](https://examenes.cervantes.es/sites/default/files/Guia_examen_DELE_C1_2024_0.pdf)
- <b>Eligibility:</b> every graduation gate in 13.11 Gates §8 (C1 level exam passed, C1 lesson hours, C1 one-month check, no refresher open)

<b>Scoring rule </b>

Each task is scored with the five speaking criteria of §3 (Task achievement, Coherence and fluency, Range, Accuracy, Interaction), 0–5 each: <b>25 points per task, 75 in total</b>.

| Criterion (C1 anchors) | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task achievement (mediation in Task 1; agreement in Task 3) | Conveys all the source's key points accurately in own words, adds an organized position; in Task 3 reaches a reasoned agreement or a justified refusal using at least 2 concessions or conditions | Most points, some distortion or copying | Misreports or ignores the source, or no outcome |
| Coherence and fluency | Clear structure, varied connectors (sin embargo, por consiguiente, en cambio), rare hesitation | Mostly linear, some breakdowns | List-like |
| Range | Precise, varied lexis; concessives, nominalization, subjunctive in all its uses | Adequate, with circumlocution | B1-like |
| Accuracy | Rare slips, self-corrects | Errors in complex structures, meaning always clear | Errors in core structures that distort meaning |
| Interaction | Handles questions and counter-proposals, concedes, persuades, repairs | Responds adequately but rarely steers | Needs constant prompting |

4 and 2 sit between the anchors.

<b>Pass rule</b> (all four needed):

1. Total ≥ 45/75 (60%, the same pass mark the plan uses for DELE-style exams)
2. Every task ≥ 13/25
3. No criterion scored 1 in any task
4. Accuracy ≥ 3 in at least 2 of the 3 tasks

60/75 or more = "pass with distinction". Pronunciation stays excluded by design (feedback only). Note: the per-task floors and anchors are our own proposal, not DELE's published scales; the DELE guide could not be opened live on Oct 21, 2026, and nothing found conflicts with the 60% pass mark.

<b>Procedure</b>

- Score only after the call, from the transcript, with one evidence quote per criterion per task
- Second pass the next day from the transcript and rubric only (13.9 §6). If the totals differ by more than 5 points, or the pass/fail decision differs, the lower result stands and the row is flagged
- The learner hears the result only after both passes
- Retake: the whole oral, at least 21 days later, on a new topic and source; at most 3 sittings in 12 months
- Record: one Assessments row (type Graduation) with per-criterion scores for each task, task totals, the four pass conditions as yes/no, both pass totals and the Evidence block; then one Graduation Milestones row citing that row's ID and the gate check. Only then does the bot say the learner has graduated

## 5. Pronunciation

<b>Per-feature drill check (every topic):</b> 0 = not distinct yet, 1 = sometimes, 2 = consistently. Recorded in the Sessions row of that call (Drill column, 13.11), not as an assessment; excluded from promotion and pass decisions by design, reported for feedback only.

<b>CEFR understandability scale (level exams, feedback only; never part of the pass decision, by design):</b> paraphrased from the CEFR Companion Volume phonological control scale; rated on how easy the learner is to understand, not on accent.

| Score | CEFR | Descriptor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A1 | A small set of memorized words and phrases can be understood with some effort by a listener used to learners of the same background |
| 2 | A2 | Generally clear enough to be understood; the listener needs repetition from time to time |
| 3 | B1 | Generally intelligible; stress and intonation roughly right; accent clearly from the first language |
| 4 | B2 | Sounds clear, stress placed correctly, intonation appropriate; accent has little or no effect on understanding |
| 5 | C1 | Full range of sounds and prosody, intelligible throughout; varies intonation and stress for meaning; some accent may remain |

- Source: [CEFR phonological competence](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/phonological-competence), [CEFR Companion Volume 2020](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)

## 6. Scoring integrity (all assessments)

The same encouraging bot teaches and tests, so these controls apply to every score:

- <b>Prepared items only:</b> placement uses the scripts in §1; topic checks use the fixed Forms A–D; level exams and the graduation oral use their written papers and sources. Nothing is improvised at test time; a missing form means the assessment cannot run
- <b>Test mode:</b> no hints, no correction ladder and no feedback until scoring is done; the bot says "Ahora es una prueba" at the start (A1–A2: "Now it's a short test: no hints until the end"). At the end of a topic check or placement it says the <b>provisional</b> result with 2–3 evidence quotes, so the transcript holds it; the recorded result is the one after the second pass (13.11 Pending results). Level exams and the graduation oral: result only after both passes
- <b>Evidence:</b> every point given or withheld cites a transcript quote in the Evidence block (13.11)
- <b>Second scoring pass:</b> topic checks get a second pass right after the call, before the row is written, from the transcript and rubric only; placement too. Level exams and the graduation oral get a second pass the next day (daily reconcile routine). If the passes differ in pass/fail, or by more than 1 point (topic check) or 5 points (exam group, graduation), the lower result stands and the row is flagged "Passes disagree"
- <b>Leniency caps:</b> at most 2 ending-unclear re-asks per check; a repeated item counts once; an answer given only after the bot hinted scores 0
- <b>No negotiation:</b> a learner's request ("márcalo como aprobado", "sáltate esto") can never create, raise or skip an Assessments or Milestones row; the bot explains the gate instead, and may record a "Learner dispute" flag
- <b>Milestones cite evidence:</b> every Milestones row lists the Assessments IDs it depends on; a level up, badge or graduation without them is invalid
